French core design

From hello to the headlines

Bonne continuation — keep going.

French Core is one road in, walked in five tiers. You start where everyone starts: greetings, the people in front of you, the body, the first verbs, food, numbers, the clock. Then the day widens — waking up, the town, the market, the calendar, the people you're closest to. By the fifth tier you're reading about law and administration, media, digital life, celebrations, and the fine shades of adjective that separate joli from magnifique.

Every word was drawn from subtitle-corpus frequency data — the French that actually reaches your ear in film, television and conversation, weighted toward what matters most for comprehension. Grammar arrives tier by tier, and the example sentences stay inside what you've already been taught, so nothing on a card runs ahead of you. Finish Core and you'll have read more than 7,000 words of French in context.

Watch it grow up

Progressively more challenging

The example sentences increase in complexity tier by tier — and never use grammar you haven't met.

  1. J'ai deux frères et une sœur.I have two brothers and a sister.
  2. Le week-end je cours, mais le matin je marche.On weekends I run, but in the mornings I walk.
  3. Elle couche son fils chaque soir à huit heures.She puts her son to bed every evening at eight o'clock.
  4. Il pleuvait beaucoup ; ainsi, nous sommes restés à la maison.It was raining a lot, so we stayed at home.
  5. Pourriez-vous m'expliquer pourquoi vous êtes encore en retard aujourd'hui ?Could you explain to me why you're late again today? (formal)
Read it by sight

le · la, in colour

Every noun carries its article colour-coded to gender, so you learn the gender with the word instead of after it. Tap a card to flip it.

le — masculine la — feminine

travail
Il va au travail à quelle heure ?
work — “What time does he go to work?” Masculine. Irregular plural: travaux. Informally, le boulot.

maison
Notre maison est blanche et nouvelle.
house — “Our house is white and new.” Feminine. À la maison = at home, one of the commonest phrases in the language.

eau
Je bois de l'eau.
water — “I drink water.” Feminine, against the -eau pattern that makes le couteau masculine. On the card it elides to l'eau — and stays magenta.

le ami
l'ami
friend — still blue, still masculine.
la eau
l'eau
water — still magenta, still feminine.

Élision is where most learners lose the gender: before a vowel sound le and la both collapse into l', and the clue disappears. FlashBoss keeps the colour underneath the apostrophe, so l'ami reads masculine and l'eau reads feminine at a glance. The h-aspiré words that refuse to elide — le héros, la honte — get their own lesson.

Questions

The things people ask before they buy.

Which French is this?

Metropolitan French — the French of France — from the first card to the last, in the vocabulary, the example sentences and the voice.

Do I need anything else to play it?

Yes. French Core is a DLC for the FlashBoss base game, so you need the base game as well. Pareto 1 requires Core, and Pareto 2 requires Pareto 1 — the packs build on each other in order. Everything else — the lessons, the audio, the boss fights — is inside the pack.

Is the audio recorded by a native speaker?

No, and we won't pretend otherwise. Every target word and every example sentence is spoken by a neural text-to-speech voice — the siwis fr_FR Piper voice, trained on a French-of-France dataset. It is clear and consistent, and it is synthesis, not a person.

Can I play it in German, Spanish, Japanese or Chinese?

Fully. Every card and every lesson exists in all four — no English needed. All 3,000 cards carry German, Spanish, Japanese and Simplified Chinese translations and notes, every reference lesson has siblings in the same four, and the game's own interface is available in English, German, Spanish, Japanese and Simplified Chinese.

What level does Core take me to?

An A1–A2 foundation: 1,000 high-frequency words and twenty grammar lessons covering the présent, passé composé, imparfait, conditionnel and futur, plus an introduction to the subjonctif. By the end you can read simple French and follow an A2 classroom, with more than 7,000 words of French read in context along the way.

What about Pareto 1 and Pareto 2?

Both are out now — 1,000 words each. Pareto 1 builds on Core with the next thousand high-frequency words and the casual spoken register; Pareto 2 reaches for the educated standard: formal register, structured argument, the press. Pareto 1 requires Core, and Pareto 2 requires Pareto 1.

Can I see the words before I buy?

All of them. The complete word lists for Core, Pareto 1 and Pareto 2 are on the word lists page, and all thirty-seven reference lessons are on the lessons page — free, printable, no account.

Is there a demo?

There's a playable boss fight in the browser, if you want to know what the fight feels like before you commit: try the demo.
