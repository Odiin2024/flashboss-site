Latin course design

One skill: reading

Legendō discitur — it is learned by reading.

Nothing here asks you to compose Latin of your own. The whole course is built so that you can look at a printed line and know what it says — and it gets you there by putting you in front of sentences from the very first tier, not by making you wait until the grammar is finished.

The order is the argument. Two thousand lemmas are laid down in ten tiers, each tier eight clusters of twenty-five cards, and the grammar arrives one lesson at a time at the exact card that first needs it. No example sentence ever uses grammar you have not been taught. By Tier 10 the ladder has nothing left to teach and you are reading Cicero unadapted — 17,007 words of Latin read in context along the way.

The macrons stop at the headword. Example sentences are printed unmarked, the way every real Latin text you will ever open is printed — so what you practise reading is the thing itself, not a teaching aid. The card tells you the vowel length; the sentence makes you carry it.

Questions

The things people ask before they buy.

When can I buy it?

Latin Core arrives on Thursday 3 September 2026 and Latin Pareto 1 on Thursday 10 September 2026. Both word lists and all thirty-nine lessons are already readable here, free, if you want to judge the course before either one is on sale.

Which pronunciation is this?

Classical — the restored pronunciation of Caesar's Rome, in the lessons and in the voice alike: c and g always hard, v as English w, Caesar said as the German Kaiser. An ecclesiastical voice is planned separately, and Church Latin proper is a branch of its own rather than an option inside this course.

Do I need anything else to play it?

Yes. Latin Core is a DLC for the FlashBoss base game, so you need the base game as well. Pareto 1 requires Core — the packs build on each other in order. Everything else — the lessons, the audio, the boss fights — is inside the pack. On Windows 10 you also need Windows Terminal — a free, secure download from the Microsoft Store.

Is the Latin real, or written for the course?

Both, in a stated order. Tiers 1 to 3 are constructed to the grammar you have been taught — no real author writes at that ceiling, and the cards claim none. Caesar enters at Tier 4, lightly trimmed. From Tier 8 on, almost every sentence is an author's own unaltered words. Across the two packs 1,052 of 2,000 sentences carry a citation and 813 of those are unadapted.

Can I play it in German, Spanish or Japanese?

Fully. Every one of the 2,000 cards carries its translation, its example translation and its study notes in German, Spanish and Japanese as well as English, with no gaps anywhere. All thirty-nine reference lessons exist in those four and in Simplified Chinese, and the game's own interface is available in English, German, Spanish, Japanese and Simplified Chinese.

What does the course get me to?

Reading unadapted classical prose with a dictionary beside you. Two thousand of the highest-frequency lemmas, the whole grammar of the indicative and subjunctive, participles, the infinitive constructions, the gerund and gerundive — and 17,007 words of Latin read in context on the way there. Not speaking Latin, and not writing it: the course is honest that it teaches one skill.

Why is there no Pareto 2?

Because Latin forks instead. Core plus Pareto 1 is a complete 2,000-word trunk, grammar-complete at Tier 10. The third thousand depends on where you are going — classical poetry or ecclesiastical Latin — so it is planned as two branches rather than one more staircase. Neither is built yet.

Can I see the words before I buy?

All of them. The complete word lists for Core and Pareto 1 are on the word lists page, and all thirty-nine reference lessons are on the lessons page — free, printable, no account.

Is there a demo?

There's a playable boss fight in the browser, if you want to know what the fight feels like before you commit: try the demo.
