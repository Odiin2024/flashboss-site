Why Swahili is learnable

A language with no irregular verbs to speak of

Swahili is the working language of East Africa — Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi and the eastern Congo — and it is spoken by far more people who learned it than by people born to it. That has worn it smooth. Spelling is exactly as it sounds, stress is always the second-to-last syllable, and there is no tone and no grammatical gender.

What it has instead is structure you can see. The verb is a train of slots. The noun carries a class, and the class rides through the whole sentence. Neither is hidden, and neither has a list of exceptions waiting for you at intermediate level.

This course teaches ordinary Swahili — the language of the market, the school and the news. The frequency backbone it is built from was made for that, not for subtitles.

The verb is an assembly

Slots, in a fixed order

Three sentences from the pack's own cards, taken apart. The order never changes: who, when, whom, what.

who — the subject when — the tense whom — the object what — the root
anaendesha
Baba yangu anaendesha basi la shule kila asubuhi, na mama anaendesha gari. — My father drives the school bus every morning, and my mother drives the car.
a-he / she na-present endeshadrive, make go
Three pieces, read left to right: he · now · drives. The root itself is built — endesha is the causative of kwenda, to go, so it means to make something go.
tulisafiri
Tulisafiri kwa treni kutoka jijini hadi kijijini kwetu wakati wa likizo. — We travelled by train from the city to our village during the holiday.
tu-we li-past safiritravel
Change one letter in the middle slot and you change the tense. tunasafiri is we are travelling; tutasafiri is we will travel. Nothing else in the word moves.
walipanda
Watu wengi walipanda basi hilo kubwa mapema asubuhi ya leo. — Many people boarded that big bus early this morning.
wa-they, class m/wa li-past pandaclimb, board, plant
The subject slot is not just a pronoun: it agrees with the class of the noun. Watu is class m/wa, so the verb starts wa-. That is the second machine.

The pack drills this directly. One of Swahili's three revision drills is the verb train, built slot by slot — you assemble the form rather than recall it whole.

The engine

Every noun carries its class

Swahili has no gender. It has classes — and the class of the noun decides the shape of its plural, its adjectives, its verb and its possessives. Get the class and the agreement comes free.

m/wa
people — mtu / watu
m/mi
trees, living things, body parts
ji/ma
large things, pairs, groups
ki/vi
objects, tools, languages
n/n
loans, animals, many abstracts
u/n
abstract nouns, mass nouns
u/ma
long thin things
u/u
a smaller set, no plural shift
ku
the infinitive used as a noun
pa
place

Those are the ten classes this pack actually uses, taken from its cards rather than from a grammar. The class is printed on every single noun in the deck, where another course would leave you to infer it.

Do I need the base game?

Yes. Swahili Core is DLC for FlashBoss, so you need the base game as well. Everything else — the lessons, the audio, the drills, the boss fights — is inside the pack. On Windows 10 you also need Windows Terminal, a free download from the Microsoft Store.

Can I study it in my own language?

Fully. Every card carries its translation, its example translation and its study notes in German, Japanese, Russian, Simplified Chinese and Spanish as well as English, with no gaps, and all 20 reference lessons carry the same six. The game's own interface speaks them too.

Which Swahili is this?

Standard Kiswahili — the one taught in schools and used by the press across East Africa, based on the Zanzibar dialect. The voice is a neural standard Kiswahili voice.

Can I see the words before I buy?

All of them. The complete word list and all twenty reference lessons are on this site — free, printable, no account. There is also a playable boss fight.

Is there a British or American spelling layer?

That layer covers the English packs. Swahili's English is the translation side of the card, and the pack ships one edition of it.
