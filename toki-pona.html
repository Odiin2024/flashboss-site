Toki Pona core design

The whole language, not a slice

toki pona — the language of good.

Toki Pona is a constructed language built as an experiment in minimalism: around 140 words, and a question underneath them — how much can you say with almost nothing? There are no tenses to conjugate, no genders to memorise, no irregular anything. Spelling is phonetic: say what you see, and you have said it right.

What is left is combination. tomo tawa, a moving structure, is a car. ilo toki, a talking tool, is a telephone. And because the vocabulary is so small, each word has to stretch: pona is good, simple, useful and kind at once. You stop reaching for the exact word and start deciding what you actually meant — which is the point of the whole exercise.

Most languages are too big to finish. This one is not. FlashBoss teaches all of it in 500 cards across 22 clusters and 5 tiers: 178 single-word entries — every content word and every grammar particle the course teaches, the book vocabulary plus the community words that have earned their place beside it — 250 compound phrases, and 72 sentences built from them.

Progressively more challenging

The example sentences increase in complexity tier by tier.

Build it from parts

Two small words, one new one

There are no genders to colour-code here — so FlashBoss shows you the thing that actually matters. A modifier always follows the word it modifies, and the pair means something neither half does alone. Two hundred and fifty cards in this pack are exactly that.

pi

Regroups the words after it into one bunch, so a later describing word sticks to the whole group rather than only the last word — jan pi toki pona is a person of Toki Pona. “This is a Toki Pona speaker.”

Sets the scene: everything before la is the when, the if, or the as-for of what follows. The language's all-purpose context marker. “As for me, I'm fine.”

A raccoon, or a related animal in the raccoon and weasel family. Coined as an April Fools' joke in 2009 — the longest word in a language built on shortness. It stuck, and it is now one of the community's best-loved words.

It is synthesised speech, not a recording of a speaker.
Questions

Before you start

Is Toki Pona really free?

Yes. It is one of the packs inside the base game — there is no Toki Pona DLC to buy, and no upgrade behind the later tiers. All 500 cards and all 13 lessons are there from the first launch.

What exactly is in the pack?

500 cards over 22 clusters and 5 tiers: 178 single-word entries — every content word and every grammar particle the course teaches — plus 250 compound phrases and 72 whole sentences. You can read the entire word list before you start, on the word lists page.

Does it cover the community vocabulary as well as the book?

It does. The spine is the book vocabulary, and tier 5 carries the later words that stuck — kijetesantakalu, tonsi, misikeke — before closing on two clusters of bonus vocabulary from further out still: majuna (old), linluwi (a network), isipin (to ponder), nimisin (a new or joke word). The final lesson explains the difference between pu and ku, so you always know which room you are standing in.

Do I need to know English to use it?

No. Cards, lessons and the interface all run in English, German, Japanese and Spanish. Every one of the 500 cards carries its translation, its example translation and its notes in all four, and all 13 lessons exist in all four.

Where does the grammar come in?

At the door. There are 13 lessons and each one fires at the exact card where its particle first turns up — li arrives with jan, pi with pi, la with ken la. Eight of them land in tier 1. You can also read them all in advance on the lessons page.

Can I hear it?

Yes — through text-to-speech, not a recorded voice. Toki Pona has fourteen sounds and the pack speaks them through FlashBoss's native Esperanto voice, whose phoneme set covers all fourteen exactly, with the penultimate stress the language expects.

Do I have to finish another pack first?

No. Toki Pona has no prerequisite. It is a standalone course, and small enough to sit alongside whatever you are already learning without taking the room.
