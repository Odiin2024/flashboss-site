where science keeps its names

The layer above Latin

When English needs to name something precisely, it reaches for Greek.

English does its everyday work in Germanic words and its arguing in Latin — but its naming is Greek. Telescope, democracy, xenophobia, chromosome, bradycardia: every one assembled from a small kit of Greek parts, the same few pieces recombined across technology, politics, biology, and medicine.

The kit is small and ruthlessly regular. Learn -phobia once and claustrophobia and xenophobia open together. Learn -ology and you hold the name of every science at once. This is the vocabulary of textbooks, journals, and hospital charts — the register where precision is the whole point — and it is built to be read, not memorised.

Thirteen words, one root — running from the sofa to the clinic. The path that feels-with in sympathy is the same one that suffers in neuropathy. See it once, and both ends of the register belong to you.
